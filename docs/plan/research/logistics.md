# logistics

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ Household pickups can't pay for hired transport: Porter's cheapest Bengaluru mini-truck starts at ₹230 a trip, over ₹9/kg for a 25 kg household lot when newspaper fetches ₹10/kg. So Luma.Green should batch household pickups on kabadiwalas' own vehicles and put its logistics features into pooled kabadiwala-to-yard loads, with verified weights and e-way bill checks.

## Recommendations

- **Pickup time slots with a limit per kabadiwala, plus a minimum load** (prototype-now): Missed and late pickups are where household apps fail, and each pickup takes 45–60 minutes. Offer 2-hour windows. Each kabadiwala sets how many pickups fit in a window (default 2 per vehicle), and full windows disappear. If the AI estimate is under the admin's minimum (default 15 kg, as The Kabadiwala uses), suggest a drop-off with the shop code instead. All these numbers live in one admin-editable serviceRules table with start dates, like the price tables.
- **Today's route for the kabadiwala, and 'on the way' with an ETA for the household** (prototype-now): Mapbox is already planned. Its Optimization API v1 orders up to 12 stops per call and is free for the first 100,000 calls a month. One button orders the day's accepted pickups and opens navigation. While the kabadiwala is on the way, the tracking page shows an ETA from his phone; location sharing stops once the pickup is done. A strong demo for little code.
- **Vehicle fit from the photo estimate** (prototype-now): Scrapr already makes households pick a vehicle for their load; Luma.Green can do it automatically. It matches the estimated kg and bulk (loose or bundled) to a handcart, cycle, auto or mini-truck, using an editable vehicleTypes table (payload kg, rough volume, fares). Only kabadiwalas whose registered vehicle fits are offered, and heavy lots never go to a handcart.
- **Pooled yard collection: fill one vehicle from several nearby kabadiwalas** (prototype-now): This is the hand-off the founder called most important. The yard sees nearby stock on a map and picks shops until a capacity bar for the chosen vehicle (kg and volume) is full. It gets a suggested stop order and collection window, and each kabadiwala accepts or declines. A 'yard collects / kabadiwala delivers' switch and a 'who pays freight' field cover both patterns seen in Bengaluru. Pooling tackles empty running (28–43%) and light materials that fill a vehicle by volume. This answers questions 4, 5 and 9 in docs/product/kabadiwala-to-yard.md.
- **Freight estimate from an admin rate table, booked with the yard's own transporter or a partner** (prototype-now): Porter publishes only starting fares (₹150–330 in Bengaluru) and no public API. So keep an editable fare per vehicle type (base fare, ₹/km, loading charge) and show an estimate per load and per kg. Booking stays with the yard's usual transporter or a partner app. A real partner integration waits until volumes justify it.
- **Weight slip when a load leaves and when it arrives, for every business hand-off** (pilot): Record the kabadiwala's weight when the load leaves and the yard's weight on arrival (weighbridge or platform scale). Add a slip photo, the vehicle number, gross, tare and net weights, and a deduction line with a reason. Flag gaps above an editable tolerance. Also store each scale's Legal Metrology stamp expiry and send a reminder 30 days before its yearly re-verification. Verra, EPR buyers and Recykal-style document packs expect this, and it heads off weight disputes.
- **Paperwork checklist for each load (delivery challan, e-way bill, reverse charge)** (pilot): From the load's value, vehicle and material, tell each side what the law expects. A handcart, or a weighbridge trip of up to 20 km with a delivery challan, needs no e-way bill. Above ₹50,000 in Karnataka an e-way bill is needed, and the registered buyer generates it when buying from an unregistered kabadiwala. Metal scrap bought from unregistered sellers carries reverse-charge GST. Keep these rules in an admin table by state with start dates, and export the fields. Filing stays in the yard's GST software or with a GST filing provider later.
- **Vehicle, driver and live status for each bulk load** (pilot): Capture the vehicle number and driver's phone at booking and a photo at loading. Share the phone's location only while the load is moving. Statuses run scheduled → loading → on the way → weighed on arrival. Recykal sells this (GPS, ETA, driver contact); Luma.Green can do a simple version without tracking hardware. The SWM Rules 2026 already require tracked vehicles for city collection contracts.
- **Admin-edited road restrictions and safe loading rules** (pilot): Bengaluru's September 2026 notices bar goods vehicles from several elevated roads by vehicle type and hour, and routing services don't know this. A small roadRestrictions table (road, vehicle types, hours, start and end dates, source link) warns when a collection window clashes. Add a loading checklist: never plan beyond the vehicle's rated payload, cover open loads, gloves and shoes for loaders.
- **Guardrail for special-care waste (batteries, e-waste)** (pilot): Batteries need authorised transport under CPCB's 2026 guidelines: charge limits, sealed containers, advance notice to the pollution board, manifests. Tag these materials in the catalogue, keep them out of normal pickups, and point households to authorised collectors or brand take-back points.
- **Route planning across several vehicles, and partner freight for yard-to-recycler loads** (scale): Once yards run several vehicles a day, move from Mapbox v1 to VROOM, a free open-source engine on our own server (BSD-2 licence) that handles time windows and capacities. Connect a freight partner for full-truck loads (Porter within the city, Vahak-style load boards between cities). Luma.Green keeps the weights, documents and trail; partners own the trucks and drivers.
- **Compliance exports: SWM Rules 2026 returns and gig welfare-fee reports** (scale): When kabadiwalas serve bulk generators or Saathis do paid pickups, the same records can produce two reports. One is the quarterly SWM return (quantity per generator and per receiving facility). The other is Karnataka's quarterly welfare-fee declaration (payout and vehicle type per job). Build them as exports once the obligations are confirmed.

## Risks

- Kabadiwalas may find household pickups not worth it (45–60 minutes each), then skip small bookings. No-shows are the main complaint about existing apps. Minimum loads, drop-off as the default, and limits per slot reduce the risk but don't remove it.
- If Luma.Green books vehicles or dispatches Saathis, it may count as an aggregator under Karnataka's gig-workers law (1% fee, quarterly declarations). It could also be drawn into accident or damage claims. Keep transport contracts between the parties and their transporters.
- The paperwork checklist could be wrong or go out of date: e-way bill thresholds, reverse charge on metal scrap, state rules. Users could then face penalties. Label it as guidance, keep the rules in dated admin tables, and have a chartered accountant review them before the pilot.
- Recording weights at both ends will surface gaps from moisture, contamination and scale error. Without an agreed tolerance and deduction reasons, the numbers can create disputes instead of trust.
- Temporary road bans and lane-level access in Bengaluru aren't in routing services, so promise time windows, not exact times.
- Porter and other freight apps publish only starting fares and no public API. Freight estimates will be rough until pilot yards share real per-km charges.
- Safety: lithium-battery fires, lifting injuries, overloaded vehicles, and Saathis entering homes. The SWM Rules 2026 expect PPE and training for collection and transport workers.
- Location privacy: kabadiwala and driver locations must be shared only during an active job and deleted after, in line with the data-protection page.

## Open questions

- In Bengaluru, do yards usually collect with their own vehicles or do kabadiwalas deliver? Who pays the freight? (Ask pilot yards.)
- What do pilot yards and local transporters actually charge per km and for loading? Porter publishes only starting fares.
- What is the smallest load per material worth a yard collection, by kg and by volume?
- Will yards generate e-way bills in their own GST software, or does Luma.Green need a GST filing partner? Is the SMS e-way bill enough for small yards?
- Will the Greater Bengaluru Authority require kabadiwalas, or Luma.Green, to register under the SWM Rules 2026 before collecting from bulk generators (e.g. apartment societies producing over 100 kg a day)?
- Is Luma.Green an 'aggregator' under Karnataka's gig-workers law if Saathis do pickups but are paid by kabadiwalas?
- Have the Legal Metrology penalties listed by Invest Karnataka (Sep 2025) changed since?
- Can a startup use ULIP, the government's logistics data platform (vehicle records, driving licences, FASTag data), to check transporters' vehicles and drivers? Not checked in this research.
- What are Bengaluru's standing, city-wide entry hours for goods vehicles? Only the temporary September 2026 notices were checked.
