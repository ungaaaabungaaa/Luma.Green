# trust-safety

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ Scrap fraud happens where the records stop matching the physical material. In May 2026 Karnataka's tax department busted 127 shell firms that had invoiced ₹2,384 crore of scrap that never moved. Luma.Green's trust edge is making every hand-off provable: a verified person, a scale checked under the Legal Metrology law, a photo taken in the app and a receipt the household confirms. Most of this can be shown to investors within weeks.

**Fraud and safety risks, and Luma.Green's control for each.** Stage: prototype-now = can be demoed in weeks; pilot = needed for Bengaluru, Oct 2026; scale = India-wide later.

## Recommendations

- **Proof of pickup: a handover code, a photo of the scale reading, and a receipt the household confirms** (prototype-now): This one feature closes the four cheapest frauds: fake pickups, farming recycle points, light scales and short cash payments. It uses data the booking flow already has. The household's tracking page (/b/{code}) shows a 4-digit code that the kabadiwala must enter to complete the pickup. The kabadiwala takes one photo of the scale display per material, through the app's camera only, and the server records the time and the phone's location. The household then gets an SMS receipt (kg × rate for each material) with a one-tap 'I got ₹X' or 'Something's wrong'. Under-weighing by a buyer is an offence under Legal Metrology Act s.30(c)/s.33, and Bengaluru Urban filed 5,375 weights-and-measures cases in 17 months. It needs one new SMS template registered on DLT (the SMS template registry).
- **Trust badges on every business card: phone → documents → scale → site** (prototype-now): This turns the admin's checklist into something households and yards can see. 'Scale verified' means the admin looked up the kabadiwala's Legal Metrology certificate on e-Mapan (free) and recorded its number and expiry date, with a reminder before it expires. 'Site verified' means a visit, or a recorded, location-stamped live video walk-through modelled on RBI's video-KYC rules, so one admin can cover yards outside Bengaluru. Store the badge rules as rows in a table so they can change without new code.
- **Weight-trust and anomaly view in the admin console** (prototype-now): The pilot already stores the estimated and weighed kg for each pickup. Comparing each kabadiwala's weighed-to-estimated ratio with the city median for each material spots steady under-weighing and fake volume. It is the same plausibility logic that exposed the ₹2,384 crore shell network and the 600,000 excess EPR certificates. A flag should trigger a test pickup and a conversation, never an automatic suspension.
- **Complaints and disputes with deadline timers** (prototype-now): Add a 'Something's wrong' button with categories: weight, payment, behaviour, no-show and safety. The photos, receipt and location are attached automatically. The complaint is acknowledged within 48 hours and resolved within 30 days, the standard in the E-Commerce Amendment Rules 2026 (in force 1 Jan 2027). A named grievance officer is shown in the app. This reuses the existing application SLA pattern ('Due soon' / 'Overdue') and the audit log, so the build is small.
- **Grade cards: one admin-editable table of material rules** (pilot): One table per material sets its grade code and allowed moisture and contamination; the ISRI defaults are paper at 12% moisture or less, and corrugated boxes at 1% prohibited material and 5% off-grade plus prohibited. It also marks materials at high risk of theft (copper cable, manhole covers), marks hazardous ones with a PPE list, and says who may buy them (batteries and e-waste only to authorised recyclers). Buyers who deduct weight must attach a photo and a reason, and the seller sees it. Because it is data, not code, the founders can change it any time.
- **Safety pack for home pickups** (pilot): This answers open question 5. Saathis and helpers who visit homes need a Karnataka Police Verification Certificate from Seva Sindhu. Other pieces: the person's name, photo and badge on the tracking page; calls through masked numbers; share-trip and an SOS button; and a strike-based suspension policy. Accept BBMP waste-picker occupational ID cards (10,437 issued since 2011) as ID, and recruit Saathis through Hasiru Dala.
- **Identity checks that never handle an Aadhaar number** (pilot): For the pilot, prefer a driving licence, voter ID, PAN, a DigiLocker document or a BBMP waste-picker card. Accept a masked Aadhaar only after its Secure QR signature has been verified, as UIDAI reg. 16C requires, and never store the number (reg. 14A). Selfie matching against the ID stays with the admin. At scale, register Luma.Green itself as an OVSE (reg. 13A); a KYC vendor cannot verify on its behalf (reg. 16A(2)). Then accept Aadhaar App credentials with offline face verification, which prove name, photo and 18+ without the number.
- **Saathi safety, child-labour and fair-removal rules** (pilot): Job cards list the hazards and required PPE; the CPCB rule for damaged batteries is gloves, eyewear, apron and shoes, and Saathis never drain acid or dismantle. Add an incident button and first-aid guidance. Check 18+ from the date of birth on the ID. Businesses declare that no one under 14 works for them and no one under 18 works in listed processes (batteries, e-waste, plastics, paper). A report button reaches the admin and links to PENCIL. Before removing a Saathi, give written reasons and 14 days' notice, as Karnataka's gig-worker law requires; only safety cases justify an immediate pause. The SWM Rules 2026 already require PPE and training for collection staff.
- **Two-way ratings tied to confirmed transactions** (pilot): Ratings can only come from a pickup completed with the handover code, one per booking, in both directions: household to kabadiwala and kabadiwala to household or Saathi. This makes fake reviews from spare SIM cards useless. The pilot's two-question follow-up SMS becomes the rating. Publish a plain-language page on how ranking works, as the 2026 e-commerce rules expect.
- **Integrity rules for B2B hand-offs, and escrow release** (scale): Record both sides' weights, with no adjustment inside a ±1% band. Require a photo of the certified weighbridge slip (gross, tare, net, vehicle number) above a threshold. Check each GSTIN live on the GST portal (active, returns filed). Generate invoices only from recorded hand-offs, and flag volumes above the business's declared capacity. Release escrow after a quality-check window and track the MSMED 45-day payment deadline; ₹31,690 crore is stuck across 112,929 small-business cases. This is also what makes later carbon or EPR claims believable.
- **Stolen-material alerts to buyers** (scale): Verified police or utility accounts (for example BESCOM, the city electricity utility) post alerts that reach every kabadiwala and yard within a set radius, and sellers of flagged categories must give ID and a photo. ReMA's ScrapTheftAlert shows the model works: 26,705 alerts, 38,209 users and about $4.0 million recovered.
- **AI photo grading of yard lots** (scale): Camera-based recognition is proven at facility scale: Greyparrot analysed 52 billion objects across 115+ categories in 2025, replacing sampling that covers under 0.1% of material. Use it for bale quality checks and as evidence in grade disputes at yards. For households the AI stays estimate-only, and prices always come from the price tables.

## Risks

- Holding ID documents and selfies is a liability. A leak, or a stray unmasked Aadhaar upload, becomes a reportable incident: to UIDAI within 72 hours if Luma.Green registers as an OVSE, and to CERT-In within 6 hours. Collect as few documents as possible and delete them on schedule.
- One admin cannot verify, investigate and settle disputes at India scale. Decisions will be slow and inconsistent. Having the same person set price floors and judge disputes is also a conflict of interest.
- Fraud flags can be wrong. The AI weight estimate has material-specific errors, so a comparison score can wrongly accuse honest kabadiwalas. Suspending people without due process damages supply, and for Saathis may breach the Karnataka law's 14-day notice rule.
- Evidence can be faked: gallery photos, edited photo metadata, GPS-spoofing apps and handover codes read out over the phone. Allow in-app camera capture only, use server timestamps, and never treat a single signal as proof.
- Luma.Green could be held liable, legally or in reputation, for theft, harassment or injury during a home pickup. The pilot has no insurance.
- Acting as a channel for stolen metal or fake invoices invites police and GST scrutiny, especially once invoices or e-way bills are generated through the platform.
- Saathi jobs may make Luma.Green an 'aggregator' under Karnataka's gig-worker law and the national Social Security Code. That brings registration, a welfare fee and dispute committees.
- Heavy KYC and photo steps may put off kabadiwalas who are new to smartphones or have little formal schooling. Keep the steps where risk is low optional and light.
- Child labour found at a partner yard would be serious reputational damage in front of investors, and self-declarations alone are weak evidence.
- Moisture and contamination limits copied from ISRI (a US standard) may not fit Indian monsoon conditions or local trade practice. They need local calibration before they are enforced.

## Open questions

- Will UIDAI register an early-stage startup as an OVSE, at what fee, and does the Aadhaar App credential flow work from a web app/PWA (deep link or QR) rather than only from native apps?
- For the pilot, should masked Aadhaar be accepted at all, given the QR-signature check it requires? Or only a driving licence, voter ID, PAN, DigiLocker documents and BBMP waste-picker cards?
- Can a Saathi apply for the Seva Sindhu Police Verification Certificate themselves, and what are the fee and turnaround? Do a kabadiwala's helpers who visit homes need one too?
- Do the pilot kabadiwalas' scales carry valid Legal Metrology stamps today, and how many use spring balances rather than electronic scales?
- How far can the estimate and the weighed kg normally differ for each material before a flag is fair? This needs pilot data.
- Are Saathi jobs 'platform-based gig work' under the Karnataka law if the kabadiwala pays the Saathi outside the platform? If so, who pays the welfare fee?
- Which materials should count as high theft risk in Bengaluru (BESCOM copper, BWSSB manhole covers, metro and railway fittings), and would the police and utilities post alerts?
- Do the E-Commerce Rules apply when the household is the seller and the kabadiwala the buyer? Or does Luma.Green adopt the grievance norms voluntarily?
- What moisture and contamination deductions do Bengaluru yards and paper mills actually apply today? The grade-card defaults should start from these.
- Which escrow or payment partner will Luma.Green use, and what KYC will it ask of kabadiwalas who have no GST registration?
