# competitors-b2b

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ Of 18 recycling platforms reviewed, none connects household bookings, kabadiwala tools and yard-to-recycler trade on one platform that many buyers use. Recykal (₹1,498 crore gross revenue in FY26) only deals with sellers that have verified GST registration, and EPR certificates can only be traded on CPCB's regulated exchange, which charges 4% and requires its operator to earn ₹50 crore a year. So Luma.Green's opening is a traced first mile plus simple yard tools, not bulk trading or EPR credits.

Also reviewed:

- **Namo eWaste** (Faridabad): listed on the NSE; e-waste recycler with EPR services, collection and return transport, and IT equipment disposal; 1 lakh+ t a year across four facilities; no marketplace.
- **Rheaply** (US): tracks an organisation's equipment and runs an internal marketplace for reusing it, with a network of 3,400+ partners.
- **Scrap Dragon** (US): yard software that takes a photo and ID automatically at the scale, reports to metal-theft databases, tracks barcoded stock and runs dispatch.

**Gaps Luma.Green can fill:**

1. Nobody connects household bookings, kabadiwala tools and yard-to-recycler trade.
2. Small yards and Bengaluru's dry-waste collection centres have no stock book that works on a phone in Indian languages.
3. Registered recyclers and PROs need proof of where material came from in the first mile, now for non-ferrous metals too.
4. Local prices as actually paid at each hand-off, not quotes.
5. Trust for small lots through a verified badge and a weight check by both sides, instead of lab tests and escrow.

## Recommendations

- **Lot history with a shareable origin sheet** (prototype-now): Recykal, Karo Sambhav, ReCircle, Banyan Nation and ScrapCart all sell traceability, but each starts after the first mile. Show one lot (say 320 kg of newspaper) with every hand-off: the pickups it came from (area only), weights, photos, prices and times. Then offer a QR code or PDF sheet the yard can pass on to its buyer. It reads the stock-movement records already planned, and it is the clearest investor demo of what makes Luma.Green different. Call it an origin record, never an EPR certificate.
- **Two-way stock board: kabadiwalas post what they have, yards post what they want; accept or make one counter-offer** (prototype-now): Listings with negotiation are the norm (Recykal's moderated deals, Recyclaro's requests for quotes). Auctions (Recykal e-waste, ScrapCart, CPCB's exchange) suit big industrial lots, not a shop's 300 kg of paper. Letting both sides post answers the open question of who starts a trade without choosing one. Minimum quantities and search distance go in tables the admin can edit.
- **Verified badge on every business card, showing how long its consent document is valid** (prototype-now): Every B2B rival leads with verification: Recykal checks GST for every seller, Recyclaro checks identity, ScrapCart verifies its buyers. Luma.Green already checks every business by hand. Showing 'verified on <date>, pollution-board consent valid until <date>' in listings costs little and puts that trust on screen.
- **Bengaluru price board built from recorded trades** (prototype-now): ScrapMonster, Recyclaro (₹4,199 a month) and MetalMandi sell quotes, price indexes or AI prices. Luma.Green can show what was actually paid at each hand-off, for example the typical (median) ₹/kg for each material over the last 7 days, from household to kabadiwala and from kabadiwala to yard, next to the admin minimum. It comes from data the pilot records anyway (sample data can fill it for the demo) and grows into the planned live-prices module.
- **Hand-over check on both sides: weight, a photo of each material, and a dispute button** (pilot): Weight and grade checks are expected everywhere (Recykal grade testing, ScrapCart composition tests, Metaloop calibrated scales). When a kabadiwala hands a load to a yard, both sides confirm the weight (shop scale against yard weighbridge) and a photo of each material. A difference bigger than an allowance the admin sets (for example 2%) opens a dispute for the admin. The confirmed figures feed the lot history.
- **Free yard book: stock in and out, sorting, sales, photo of the weighbridge slip** (pilot): Yard software like Scrap Dragon has no simple Indian-language equivalent that works on a phone, and Hasiru Dala's system for its centres still starts on paper with monthly entry. A daily book using the same stock tables as the kabadiwala app gives yards a reason to open Luma.Green every day and produces trace data.
- **Trade documents generated from the record** (pilot): Recykal wins recyclers with a document set per trade (invoice, e-way bill, weight certificate) and stored GST records. Luma.Green can produce one PDF per trade with a delivery challan or draft invoice, weight slip and photos, add the GST number when there is one, and pre-fill the e-way bill fields for loads that need one, without filing anything.
- **Record proof of payment now; add escrow later through a licensed partner** (pilot): Escrow is standard between businesses (CPCB's bank escrow, ScrapCart, ScrapoSure), but Razorpay Route has turnover and RBI conditions. Metaloop failed once financing trades became too costly. In the pilot, record the UPI reference or a photo of the cash receipt for each trade and mark advance payments. Never pre-pay for trades, hold stock or hold anyone's money. Add Route or a bank escrow when trade volume justifies it.
- **Bring Bengaluru's dry-waste collection centres on as yards** (pilot): The centres run by waste pickers (33 supported by Hasiru Dala) already sort dry waste and sell to recyclers, and Hasiru Dala Innovations sells traceable plastic. Partnering gives the pilot real yard volume and turns a likely rival into a supplier on the platform. It needs only a 'collection centre' flag on the existing yard type, not a new role.
- **EPR evidence export for registered recyclers and PROs** (scale): Recyclers and processors create the certificates and trade them on CPCB's exchange, which does not allow brokers and requires its operator to earn ₹50 crore a year. Luma.Green's part is the evidence. It can export each lot's origin area, weights, photos, GPS and dates, sorted into CPCB's categories (plastic Cat I–III; e-waste iron, aluminium, copper, gold; non-ferrous metals from April 2026). Once the exchange is live, it can also read the exchange's published prices through its API.
- **Free for the first mile; paid plans for yards, recyclers and manufacturers** (scale): Price points to compare: Recyclaro charges ₹8,299–24,900 a month, ScrapMonster charges for live prices, Scrapo charges for its guarantee service, and CPCB's exchange takes 4% per trade. Rubicon's thin broker margin ($4.5M gross profit on $163.1M in one quarter) shows a cut of each trade may not be enough to fund the business. Charge buyers for exports, trade documents, analytics and API access, and never take a share of the kabadiwala's cash.

## Risks

- Recykal (₹1,498 crore gross revenue in FY26, $23M raised in June 2026, seller app live in Karnataka) could reach down to kabadiwalas faster than Luma.Green can reach up to bulk trade.
- Recyclers' own buying apps, such as Attero's MetalMandi, can outbid a platform that favours no single buyer for kabadiwala supply and pull traders away from it.
- Kabadiwalas prefer cash and avoid GST records, so records may be incomplete or late; any traceability claim is only as good as the data entered.
- Calling Luma.Green's origin record a 'certificate', or offering to 'sell EPR credits', would break CPCB rules: certificates are created by recyclers and processors and can only be traded on the regulated exchange, where brokers are not allowed.
- Handling money without a licensed payment partner, or pre-paying for trades, brings legal and financial risk (Metaloop entered restructuring in June 2026).
- Cheating on weight or quality at hand-over (wet paper, mixed grades) will destroy trust unless both sides check the load and there is a way to raise disputes.
- In Bengaluru, Hasiru Dala Innovations already sells fair-trade, traceable plastic to recyclers and brands; competing with it instead of partnering would split the same supply.
- EPR rules and the exchange's launch keep changing, so anything tied to them (categories, fees, price limits) must live in tables the admin can edit, not in code.
- Brokers earn thin margins (Rubicon: $4.5M gross profit on $163.1M revenue in Q2 2024), so a cut of each trade alone may not pay for running the platform.

## Open questions

- Is the MSTC-run EPR exchange live, and are certificate deals outside it now banned? An industry blog on 26 March 2026 found no launch date, while an April 2026 compliance guide says using it is compulsory in 2026. Only CPCB can confirm.
- What proof of material origin do registered recyclers and PROs in Karnataka need for CPCB and Karnataka pollution-board (KSPCB) audits, and would they pay for a lot history?
- Will Hasiru Dala, other collection-centre operators and the city's waste authority agree to keep the centres' stock records on Luma.Green?
- What do Recykal and MetalMandi charge small aggregators, and what are their minimum lot sizes and payment terms? None of this is published.
- Which Bengaluru materials (paper, PET, metals) move from kabadiwalas to yards in enough volume to show real matches in the investor demo?
- Can a young company like Luma.Green meet Razorpay Route's turnover minimums, or will escrow need a bank partner?
- Does helping businesses trade make Luma.Green a GST 'e-commerce operator' that must collect tax on each sale? (open question 12 in docs/product/open-questions.md)
