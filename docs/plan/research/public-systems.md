# public-systems

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ The Solid Waste Management Rules 2026 took effect on 1 April 2026. They require bulk generators, collectors and dry-waste sorters to register on CPCB's portal (live since 31 May 2026) and file weighed quarterly returns, and they require cities to recognise waste pickers and waste traders. Luma.Green's weighed record of every hand-off can therefore serve as the compliance and dashboard layer for Bengaluru's 5 new corporations, 369 wards and 119–164 dry waste collection centres.

## Key dates to design around

- **1 Apr 2026**: SWM Rules 2026 in force (S.O. 388(E), 27 Jan 2026).
- **31 May 2026**: CPCB portal opened for bulk-generator registration.
- **About 1 Oct 2026**: CPCB portal due for every obligated entity (six months after the rules took effect, r.16).
- **31 Dec 2026**: deadline for elections to Bengaluru's corporations.
- **About Jan 2027**: states must have counted and registered waste pickers, and RWAs, gated communities, markets and hotels must hand recyclables only to authorised waste pickers or recyclers (one year after notification).
- **31 Mar 2027**: cities' new SWM by-laws due.
- **Every quarter, by the 15th of the next quarter's first month**: collectors' and sorters' returns. **Every 30 June**: annual returns, plus the waste-picker registration upload.
- **About Oct 2027**: million-plus cities such as Bengaluru must have the infrastructure (including MRFs) in place (18 months, Schedule I).

## Recommendations

- **Ward dashboard for city officials: read-only, shown on demo data first** (prototype-now): GBA's five corporations are scored on dry waste processed and where it goes next (SS 2025-26 indicator 3.4). They lose marks when their monthly Swachhatam figures fail field checks, and GFC Component 21 gives 200–450 marks for software-based sale registers with vendor details. A ward map showing kg by material, the share sent to registered recyclers and the number of verified kabadiwalas, DWCCs and Saathis is the strongest demo for both investors and cities. Build it from the existing pickupReceipts plus an admin-editable ward table (geohash to ward), showing totals only and no personal data.
- **Hand-off ledger tagged with the 2026 waste stream, material code and each party's registration number, with a one-click quarterly-return export** (prototype-now): Rules 8(g) and 9(4) require collectors and dry-waste sorters to file quarterly returns by the 15th of the next quarter covering what they received, sorted and sent to registered processors; BWGs file annual returns by 30 June. Tag every receipt line with its stream (dry, wet, sanitary or special care), its material code and the buyer's registration number. Keep the material-to-stream mapping and all deadlines in admin-editable tables so rule changes are data edits, not code. A single button then produces the CSV or PDF each party files.
- **Compliance fields and badges on business profiles: city/CPCB portal registration no., KSPCB consent order no. with category and validity, GSTIN, Udyam/UAP no.** (prototype-now): Bulk generators and facilities may not deal with unregistered entities (r.6(g), 9(5)), and operating unregistered draws environmental compensation (r.17). The admin checks the KSPCB number by hand on XGN, which has a CAPTCHA and no API; the platform stores the expiry date and reminds before it runs out. Keep the 'consent not required' option for White-category paper baling. Show a 'Registered with <corporation>' badge to bulk-generator customers.
- **DWCC operator mode: daily weight sheet, sale register (buyer, material, kg, price) and monthly recyclables report** (pilot): This mirrors the SBM-U model MRF contract, which requires a digital inventory, daily weight sheets and sales reports. Bengaluru has 119–164 DWCCs, many on 2020 MoUs that have lapsed. Offering 2–5 Hasiru Dala-supported DWCCs a free 'yard' account gives them evidence for MoU renewal and NAMASTE resource-organisation fees (₹10,000 per DWCC per month), and gives Luma.Green real dry-waste volumes.
- **Occupational record for waste pickers and Saathis: a kabadiwala-issued receipt for every sale, a one-tap 'endorse', a printable 6-month history, and a field for the NAMASTE or city ID once issued** (pilot): NAMASTE accepts scrap-sale receipts or endorsement by a scrap shop as proof of occupation. The 2026 rules make city registration of waste pickers an annual duty, uploaded by 30 June. Bengaluru's 2013 ID cards have expired and are not being reissued, so Luma.Green receipts can help thousands of pickers get recognised.
- **Scheduled pickups for RWAs and other bulk generators, with an EBWGR evidence pack and empanelment of verified kabadiwalas** (pilot): Residential societies producing 100 kg/day or more are bulk generators. They must register on swm.cpcb.gov.in (live since 31 May 2026), use registered collectors and obtain EBWGR certificates, which only the city issues based on kg delivered to registered recyclers. SS 2025-26 indicator 3.7 checks that cities empanel every vendor serving bulk generators. Per-pickup weights plus the receiving recycler give the RWA its annual-return data and the corporation the basis to issue certificates.
- **One data-sharing MoU with a corporation or BSWML, co-signed with Hasiru Dala** (pilot): The new corporations (elections by 31 Dec 2026) must adopt SWM by-laws by 31 Mar 2027, a window to get verified kabadiwalas and DWCCs named as authorised collectors. Offer the dashboard and a monthly export shaped for Swachhatam (totals only, safe under the DPDP Act). In return, ask for the DWCC list, ward boundaries and a named SWM nodal officer (r.39(12)).
- **Checks through official APIs: GSTIN status via a GSP, and Saathi ID via DigiLocker's requester API** (pilot): A single admin verifies every applicant by hand. A GSP call confirms GSTIN status instantly. DigiLocker (through API Setu) pulls a driving licence or PAN with the user's consent, so the platform never handles Aadhaar numbers. Manual upload stays as the fallback.
- **GST helper for metal scrap: reverse-charge self-invoice and 2% TDS flags on yard purchases** (scale): Since the 54th GST Council, yards buying metal scrap from unregistered kabadiwalas pay the tax under reverse charge, and metal scrap sold between businesses carries 2% TDS. Generating the self-invoice from the hand-off receipt makes this automatic once trades and escrow run on the platform.
- **Chain-of-custody export for recyclers' EPR claims, with registered DWCCs/MRFs acting as EPR uptake points** (scale): Rule 9(7) lets registered MRFs serve as uptake points for producers and processors under EPR rules. Recyclers need proof of where material came from for EPR certificates, and CPCB's EPR portals have no public API. A CSV of origin per batch (ward, collector, kg, dates) is simple to build and valuable.
- **ONDC logistics booking for kabadiwala-to-yard loads** (scale): ONDC's logistics specifications and reference apps are open, so joining as a buyer on the network gives access to its logistics providers without building or managing a fleet.
- **Saathi welfare compliance switch: Karnataka welfare board registration, e-Shram referral, and payouts through a payment aggregator with a configurable welfare-fee rate** (scale): If Saathi work falls under Karnataka's gig-worker law, Luma.Green must register within 45 days, share worker data and pay a 1–5% fee on every payout. Building payouts through an aggregator (UPI up to ₹1 lakh per transaction) with the fee rate held as data turns the law into a setting rather than a rewrite.

## Risks

- Enforcement exposure: the 2026 rules impose environmental compensation on anyone collecting or sorting without registration (r.17). A platform that makes informal kabadiwalas visible to officials could expose them before they register. Share only totals until each business consents and holds a registration number.
- Government systems offer no APIs. The CPCB SWM portal, Swachhatam, the NAMASTE system and KSPCB XGN (CAPTCHA) are web forms or logins, so integration means exports and manual checks, and one admin's workload grows with every city.
- Bengaluru's institutions are in flux. The GBA dates from 2025, five corporations have elections due by 31 Dec 2026, and DWCC MoUs have lapsed. Our counterparts and priorities may change after the elections.
- Scheme funding may end. SBM-U 2.0 was set for 2021–26, and NAMASTE's waste-picker component was planned for FY 2024-25 to 2025-26. Anchor the pitch in the statutory 2026 rules rather than scheme money.
- Data protection: waste-picker identities (often scheduled-caste workers), household addresses and income data are sensitive. City dashboards must show totals only, with consent and purpose limits built to the DPDP standard.
- Livelihood conflict: waste-picker groups may see a platform as privatisation or middlemen taking their material. Co-design with Hasiru Dala and the union, and make sure pickers keep access to dry waste and see more income, not less.
- Public numbers conflict (DWCCs 119 vs 164; BBMP segregation 53–55% per CPCB vs 40–50% per experts). Dashboards need a source and date on every figure, and should be framed as help, so officials are not embarrassed.
- Tax exposure grows as trades move onto the platform: reverse charge and 2% TDS on metal scrap, and possible e-commerce operator obligations (open question #12 in the docs).

## Open questions

- Will GBA or BSWML treat kabadiwalas and DWCCs as 'entities involved in sorting' that must register under rule 9, and can individual kabadiwalas and DWCCs (not just bulk generators) register on swm.cpcb.gov.in yet?
- Will CPCB publish an API or a bulk-upload format for quarterly returns and EBWGR evidence once the portal covers every obligated entity (due about Oct 2026)?
- Does the NAMASTE waste-picker component continue beyond FY 2025-26? Which resource organisation is empanelled for Karnataka, and has waste-picker registration started in Bengaluru?
- What will Bengaluru's new SWM by-laws (due 31 Mar 2027) say about 'authorised' waste pickers, collectors and traders? Will they keep the national 100 kg/day threshold for bulk generators?
- Which of the five corporations would host a pilot dashboard, who is its SWM nodal officer, and exactly which fields does it report monthly to Swachhatam?
- Is SBM-Urban extended or relaunched after 2026, and how does the Swachh Survekshan 2026-27 toolkit change the dry-waste and digital-monitoring indicators?
- Is Karnataka's platform-based gig-workers law in force, and does it cover Saathi pickup and shift work?
- How many DWCCs actually operate in Bengaluru today, who runs them and under what contracts? Public figures range from 119 functioning (Sep 2025) to 164 (Nov 2025).
