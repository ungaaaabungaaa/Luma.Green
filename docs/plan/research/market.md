# market

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ India's recycling runs on an informal first mile: 1.5–4 million waste pickers, plus kabadiwalas and two or three tiers of dealers, move 60–70% of urban recyclables, and value leaks at every hop. This week a Bengaluru household gets ₹500–590/kg for copper that trades at ₹1,394–1,454/kg, about 35–42% of the wholesale price.

How to read the table:

- **Household end** is what a kabadiwala pays at the door in Bengaluru (The Kabadiwala, an undated page; scraprates.in, 28 Sep 2026), unless the row says Delhi NCR.
- **Factory end** is the price for a traded grade at a mill, smelter or recycler.
- **Household share** is the door price divided by the factory price, as a rough guide. Sorting, cleaning, grade, freight and credit explain part of every gap.
- **Volumes** for e-waste and plastics are disputed; see the findings.
- **Before use:** re-check the door prices with 10–20 Bengaluru shops before they seed the fallback table.

## Recommendations

- **Reference price shown beside the kabadiwala's price. Add a dated `referencePrices` table (city or market, material, paise per kg, source, effectiveFrom) that the admin fills weekly from MCX, BigMint and mill notices. It follows the same pattern as `priceFloors` and `fallbackRates`.** (prototype-now): In Bengaluru, door prices for copper, brass and aluminium are 35–50% of wholesale, and PET is about 23%. Showing the reference price in the kabadiwala rate card and in the admin and investor view makes the promise to "see the latest prices" visible straight away. Decide in the pilot whether households see it too.
- **Material catalogue kept as admin-editable data, with a unit (kg or piece), a value class (cash, points only, not accepted), a hazardous flag and an authorised-buyer-only flag. Rate cards also accept a price per piece.** (prototype-now): Names and grades change at every hop. Appliances are bought per piece at the door (laptop ₹250, fridge ₹350–550, air conditioner ₹2,500–3,000), which a paise-per-kg rate card cannot express. Milk covers and film are worth ₹0. Batteries, e-waste and tyres legally need authorised buyers.
- **Investor dashboard showing the Bengaluru chain in numbers: kg by material, rupees paid to households, the household's share of the factory price, hand-offs recorded and weight disputes. Add a panel of sourced national figures.** (prototype-now): Investors need the size of the market and the leak on one screen. Sourced figures include 41 Mt of ferrous scrap, 23 Mt of paper at 75% recycled fibre, 14.1 lakh t of e-waste, 7.07 Mt of textiles, 1.5–4 million waste pickers and at least ₹1.8–2.1 lakh crore of annual flow. Seeded demo data is enough for the prototype.
- **Weighing receipt that both sides confirm. On `pickupReceipts`, add an optional photo of the scale display, a household confirm tap, and an automatic flag when the weighed kg is far below the AI estimate. Show the kabadiwala scale's Legal Metrology stamp date, checked during onboarding.** (pilot): Bengaluru scrap shops have been caught with scales fitted with remote-controlled chips (2023). Bengaluru Urban saw 5,375 Legal Metrology cases in 17 months. Being cheated on weight is the household's first fear.
- **Gross, deduction and net on every business-to-business hand-off: gross grams, deduction %, reason (moisture, contamination, tare), net grams and a quality grade on kabadiwala-to-yard trades.** (pilot): Wet cardboard loses 30–50% of its value. Only about 30% of what reaches Bengaluru's dry waste collection centres is good enough to earn from, and textile cutting waste carries up to 20% contamination. Deductions are where disputes happen, and today none of them are written down.
- **A simple khata (credit ledger) on trades: amount due, due date, part payments and the cash or UPI reference. No money moves on the platform, which keeps ADR 0009 intact.** (pilot): Trade in this chain runs on cash and credit. Since October 2024, reverse-charge GST on metal scrap bought from unregistered sellers pushes working-capital strain onto yards. Recording who owes whom is the first step towards escrow later.
- **Onboard dry waste collection centres (DWCCs) and waste-picker cooperatives as sellers: a DWCC flag on kabadiwala-type organisations, recruited with Hasiru Dala.** (pilot): About 800 t/day of Bengaluru's recyclables already pass through waste pickers, and 119 centres are operating. They need buyers and fair prices: their ID cards have expired and their agreements with the city have lapsed. Partnering with them brings immediate supply to yards and avoids political pushback.
- **Guardrails on the minimum price. Each floor can be set as a percentage of the reference price, with an admin override and a suspend switch per material. Rows stay dated.** (pilot): The 2023 waste-paper crash halved collection, and in September 2026 mills cut scrap buying by 50–60%. A fixed floor can end up above the market and stop trade within weeks.
- **Only authorised buyers for hazardous streams. A yard or recycler must upload its state pollution board authorisation or EPR registration before it can buy lead-acid batteries, e-waste or tyres.** (pilot): Informal smelters and unregistered tyre-oil plants (440 of 736) outbid formal recyclers. Connecting hazardous waste to unauthorised buyers is a legal and reputational risk for the platform.
- **Chain-of-custody export for EPR and buyers: lot IDs linking pickup receipts, stock and trades with weights and dates, exported as CSV or PDF.** (scale): Recyclers need documented feedstock to claim EPR certificates, which trade at ₹10–50/kg for e-waste; plastics processors issued 2.5 Mt of certificates in 2022-23. Mills short of domestic scrap and fibre will pay for traceable supply.
- **Metal-scrap GST helper: tag tariff chapters 72–81, record each seller's GST status, show the buyer when reverse charge or 2% TDS applies, and produce a purchase voucher. Informational only, not tax advice.** (scale): From 10 October 2024, the buyer pays the GST on metal scrap bought from unregistered kabadiwalas, and business-to-business supplies carry 2% TDS. Yards will need this before they move volume onto the platform.
- **A monthly Luma.Green first-mile scrap index per city: median household price, kabadiwala-to-yard price and the spread for each material, built from the platform's receipts.** (scale): Official volumes are disputed, and metals already have BigMint and MCX indices. No first-mile price series exists. Publishing one is how the platform starts to set the industry's norms.

## Risks

- Showing factory prices to households may turn kabadiwalas against the platform. Their margin pays for sorting, transport, rent, credit and losses. Show the reference price to kabadiwalas and investors first, and test how households react in the pilot.
- Some price sources limit redistribution (MCX, BigMint, ScrapMonster). Others are unverified aggregators (scraprates.in, rate-list sites) or undated pages (The Kabadiwala). Label every reference price with its source and date, call it indicative, and never promise it.
- Market figures conflict by large factors: e-waste is 1.4 Mt by the pollution board and 6.19 Mt by NITI Aayog; plastics are 4.1 Mt officially against 9.3 Mt of pollution. The ₹1.8–2.1 lakh crore flow is our own estimate. Every investor slide must cite and define its number.
- Recording informal cash trades may raise GST questions for users (18% GST, reverse charge on metal scrap) and put them off. Keep the GST number optional and get tax advice before adding any invoice or voucher feature.
- Batteries, e-waste and tyres must go to authorised recyclers under the 2022 rules. The platform could be liable if it matches them with unauthorised buyers.
- Admin minimum prices can freeze trade in a price crash. That happened in the 2023 waste-paper slump and the steel-scrap buying cuts of September 2026.
- Waste-picker politics in Bengaluru are sensitive: the city's agreements with waste pickers have lapsed, their ID cards have expired and the waste company is under Lokayukta scrutiny. A platform seen as displacing waste pickers could meet resistance; partnering with Hasiru Dala-type groups lowers the risk.
- Weights recorded in the app are not certified weights under Legal Metrology law. The shop's stamped scale stays the legal instrument, so never market receipts as certified weights.

## Open questions

- How many kabadiwalas operate in Bengaluru, and what share of household dry waste reaches them rather than the city's waste company or the dry waste collection centres? No census was found.
- What margin does a Bengaluru kabadiwala actually keep per material after rent, transport, credit and losses? Interview 10–20 shops before households see reference prices.
- What are the usual payment terms from yard to kabadiwala in Bengaluru (cash at pickup, or 7–30 days' credit), and what are the minimum lot sizes per material?
- Which reference-price sources can be used legally and cheaply: MCX, a BigMint subscription, mill price notices?
- For investors, should we quote the pollution board's 1.4 Mt of e-waste or NITI Aayog's 6.19 Mt? Or both, with how each is measured?
- Has the replacement for the Solid Waste Management Rules 2016 come into force, and does it require kabadiwalas or aggregators to register? This was not verified here.
- Should batteries, e-waste and tyres be left out of the Bengaluru pilot entirely until the authorised-buyer check exists?
- Can recycle points reward zero-value items (glass, film, old clothes) without distorting the cash prices for other materials?
