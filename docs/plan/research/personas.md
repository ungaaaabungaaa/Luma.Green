# personas

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ About 80–85% of Bengaluru's dry waste passes through informal waste pickers and kabadiwalas, and the Solid Waste Management Rules 2026 (in force since 1 April 2026) now require bulk generators and sorting yards to register on a central portal and deal only with registered parties. Verifying informal players and keeping a weighed record of every hand-off is the one capability almost every persona needs.

## Recommendations

- **Demo world: one login per persona, plus read-only observer logins** (prototype-now): Investors judge breadth by clicking through each persona, and the founder asked for test IDs. Put Bengaluru demo data in one editable seed file so numbers are easy to change: wards, 6 kabadiwalas, 2 yards, 1 DWCC, 1 recycler, 1 manufacturer, 3 Saathis, 1 bulk generator and 1 RWA. Read-only observer logins (city, brand/auditor, CSR, lender, investor) show the non-trading personas without building new flows.
- **Material passport: trace one bale back to the household pickups it came from** (prototype-now): Brands and auditors are reeling from 700,000 fake EPR certificates, and MRAI names missing traceability as a core gap. One screen showing kilograms, price, date, place and verified badges at each hand-off (household → kabadiwala → yard → recycler → manufacturer) demonstrates the 'record every hand-off' principle.
- **Pilot numbers page for investors** (prototype-now): Build it from the existing events table (ADR 0012): kilograms and rupees through the platform, completed pickups, time to accept, estimated vs weighed kilograms, verified organisations by role, repeat households. Investors compare against players at Recykal's scale and want traction, not a feature list.
- **Registrations stored as editable data on every organisation, plus an optional 'registered sellers only' switch per buyer** (pilot): SWM 2026 (r.6(g), r.9(5)) and the EPR guidelines (6.3) bar registered entities from dealing with unregistered ones. Store each organisation's registrations as rows: GSTIN, pollution-board consent with expiry, CPCB EPR/processor number, SWM-portal or local-body registration, recognised-collector status, metal-scrap tax-deductor registration, Udyam (MSME) number. Buyers who must comply can switch on the filter without the platform shutting out unregistered kabadiwalas.
- **Bengaluru language pack and read-aloud** (pilot): Tamil, Telugu and Urdu speakers each outnumber Hindi speakers more than two to one in Bengaluru, and 50–60k waste pickers speak Bengali. Hand-check Kannada, English, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu, Hindi and Bengali first, order the language grid to match, and add read-aloud to every kabadiwala and Saathi action (140 m Indian internet users already use voice).
- **Weigh-and-pay trust kit** (pilot): Scale disputes are common: 5,375 weights-and-measures cases in Bengaluru Urban in 17 months. Record the scale's official stamp expiry at onboarding, allow a photo of the scale display per weighing, send an itemised SMS receipt (kg × rate), and add a one-tap dispute that reaches support.
- **Payment method and UPI-fee type on every recorded payment** (pilot): From 15 Oct 2026, UPI business payments above ₹2,000 cost 0.4% unless the receiver is a small merchant, and fewer than half of internet users pay digitally. Record cash / UPI person-to-person / UPI merchant, keep money off-platform, and watch whether business-to-business hand-offs slide back to cash.
- **Saathi onboarding built for real waste pickers, with an NGO partner** (pilot): Old BBMP picker IDs have expired and many migrants lack Aadhaar. Accept a voter ID, an expired picker card, or a letter from an NGO, DWCC or union, plus a selfie. Pilot with a Hasiru Dala-supported DWCC as the first yard and Saathi pool. Keep Saathi pay off-platform until lawyers confirm whether Luma.Green counts as an 'aggregator' under Karnataka's gig act and the national Social Security Code.
- **Compliance module for bulk generators and RWAs** (scale): SWM 2026 makes large offices, hotels, malls, hospitals and campuses register, use registered vendors and file returns by 30 June; RWAs have until Jan 2027. What these buyers pay for (as Hasiru Dala Innovations shows) is scheduled pickups by verified vendors, monthly kilograms by stream and proof of vendor registration.
- **Exports of the waste-picker and kabadiwala registry for cities and NGOs** (scale): Local bodies must keep a ward-wise database of waste pickers and informal collectors on the central portal, updated by 30 June each year. With consent, Luma.Green's verified kabadiwala and Saathi records and DWCC volumes can feed it, which makes the city a partner.
- **Evidence packs for brands and auditors, sold through registered processors** (scale): A read-only audit login with the audit log, photos, GPS, timestamps, weights and each counterparty's registrations, offered to brands through registered plastic processors. Luma.Green should never issue EPR certificates itself, given the fraud history.
- **Consent-based sharing of trade history with lenders** (scale): DWCCs and traders fund working capital informally (gold pledges, processors paying late). A consented export of verified trade history can later let banks and NBFCs lend to kabadiwalas and yards.

## Risks

- Luma.Green could count as an 'aggregator' under Karnataka's gig act or the national Social Security Code if it dispatches or pays Saathis. That brings registration, a 1% welfare fee, real-time worker reporting, contributions up to 5% of payouts, and a duty to communicate in Kannada.
- 'Registered-only' rules (SWM 2026 r.6(g) and r.9(5); EPR 6.3) could shut unregistered kabadiwalas out of bulk-generator and brand-linked trade. Ignoring the rules makes the platform unusable for business buyers; enforcing them too early loses the informal supply.
- IDs, selfies and home locations of migrant waste pickers, who fear eviction and harassment, could harm them if leaked. Weak security can draw data-protection penalties up to ₹250 crore.
- Recorded weights and prices can be gamed (inflated kilograms, fake pickups). If platform records ever feed credits, the 700,000 fake EPR certificates show how this can go wrong.
- The UPI fee from 15 Oct 2026 (during the pilot week) and GST reverse charge/TDS on metal scrap may push business hand-offs back to cash and off the platform, thinning the data trail.
- Bengaluru's institutions are in flux: BBMP gave way to the Greater Bengaluru Authority and five corporations, waste moved to BSWML, and DWCC agreements lapsed. City and DWCC partnerships are uncertain during the pilot.
- The 12-language promise may miss the languages the Bengaluru pilot actually needs (Tamil, Telugu, Urdu, Bengali) if those are checked last; users with low literacy need voice, not just translation.
- A single admin verifying every role within 12–24 hours will not scale once bulk generators, brands and auditors join.

## Open questions

- Does a scrap-pickup marketplace or Saathi job board fall under Karnataka's gig act ('home services' or 'e-marketplace') or the Social Security Code's definition of an aggregator?
- Which SWM 2026 registration should pilot yards (and Luma.Green) hold with GBA/BSWML, and will GBA recognise kabadiwalas as 'authorised' informal waste collectors?
- Will a Hasiru Dala-supported DWCC or the Karnataka waste-pickers' union co-run the pilot as a yard and Saathi pool?
- How many pilot kabadiwalas accept UPI, and do they stay under the ₹1 lakh a month small-merchant limit that avoids the new UPI fee?
- What evidence from informal collection would a brand's EPR auditor accept (photo, weight, GPS, counterparty registration), and would brands pay for it?
- Do Bengaluru RWAs and tech parks expect to be paid for dry waste or to pay for compliant collection, and what report format does BSWML ask of them?
- Which ID or attestation can undocumented migrant waste pickers provide, and will an NGO's attestation be enough for verification?
- What are recycle points worth, and could CSR budgets fund them?
