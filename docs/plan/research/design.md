# design

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ About 1 in 5 Indian adults cannot read (78.2% adult literacy, 2024) and 88% of India's mobile browsing is Chrome on Android, so Luma.Green's field screens must lead with pictures, numbers and voice. Yet 34 of the app's 51 buttons are only 28–36 px tall against its own 44–56 px rule, and the same rupee amount appears in five different formats across its 12 languages.

## Recommendations

- **Larger button sizes for phone screens** (prototype-now): 34 of 51 buttons are 28–36 px, below the project's own 44/48–56 px rule and Google's 48 dp. Add three sizes to the shared Button: touch (48 px), action (56 px, larger text, icon plus label) and icon-touch (48 px square). Make them the default in the household, kabadiwala, Saathi and join screens. Admin can keep shadcn's defaults, which already meet the WCAG AA minimum of 24 px.
- **One number format everywhere: lakh grouping with international digits** (prototype-now): Today one amount appears in five formats across the locales, and the English demo shows ₹125,000 instead of ₹1,25,000. Change useFormat() to format money, weights and counts with Indian (lakh) grouping and international digits, keeping the words translated. Mark amounts as left-to-right inside Urdu and Arabic text, and add a unit test per language. This matches Article 343's official numerals. Check the choice with Bengali and Marathi users in the pilot.
- **Fix failing colours, add material colours, and test contrast automatically** (prototype-now): Set the grey helper text to oklch(0.50 0 0) (5.5:1 on the grey page) and input borders to oklch(0.64 0 0) (3.36:1), and darken the lightest chart greens. Add a colour and matching text colour for each material (paper, cardboard, plastic, metal, glass, e-waste), plus success, warning and info colours. Add a unit test that fails if any text colour pair is below 4.5:1 or any icon or border pair is below 3:1. Materials should differ in lightness as well as hue, and always show an icon and a name.
- **A small set of shared building blocks for low-literacy screens** (prototype-now): Build these from existing shadcn pieces: MaterialTile (photo or picture, colour, and a 1–2 word name), IconAction (icon with a label that is always visible), QuantityStepper (plus and minus in half-kilo steps, with a big number keypad instead of free typing), MoneyAmount (large, evenly spaced digits), ListenButton, and OfflineBanner (using Next's useOffline). Keep one fixed main button at the bottom of each screen. Store labels, icon names, colours, photo ids and audio per language in one data table, so the founders can change them without code.
- **Illustration kit: Open Peeps plus commissioned Indian clothing and props** (prototype-now): Open Peeps is public domain (CC0): no credits, free to edit, and none of unDraw's or DrawKit's limits on redistribution or AI use. Commission about 20–30 extra pieces in the same line style: kurta, saree, salwar with dupatta, lungi or mundu, caps, turbans, hijab and a Saathi vest, plus a pushcart (thela), tricycle cart, platform scale, sacks, newspaper bundles, cardboard bales, PET bottles, a tempo or auto, and a warehouse shed. Recolour them with CSS variables, compress each SVG, and keep a credits file (source, licence, link, date checked). Use unDraw only on the public site. Keep Storyset (free plan), free Noun Project icons and OpenMoji out of the app.
- **A 'Hear it' button with pre-recorded audio for the demo's key screens** (prototype-now): Text screens fail first-time low-literacy users; voice plus pictures helps. Generate 10–20 prompts (how to sell, photo tips, the estimate, booking, accept or reject, weigh and pay) in Kannada, Hindi and English, using Google's Chirp 3 HD voices or the free Indic Parler-TTS. Store them as small audio files per message and language, cached on the phone. Have native speakers check how trade words like raddi and bhangar are pronounced. For the pilot, cover every prompt in every pilot language, and speak live details (weights, times, areas) with text-to-speech on the fly.
- **Automatic code fill-in for every code SMS** (pilot): Add '@luma.green #{#var#}' as the last line of the sign-in and booking code template in every language before DLT approval. On the verify screen, ask Chrome for Android to read the code, and keep the existing one-time-code setting for iPhones. This removes typing six digits, which is error-prone for new smartphone users. Check that the extra line does not push Kannada or Hindi templates into a second SMS segment.
- **Installable app that copes with dropped connections** (pilot): Add 192 px and 512 px PNG icons, adaptive (maskable) icons, screenshots, a description and home-screen shortcuts ('Sell scrap', 'Today') to manifest.ts, and ask kabadiwalas to install once. Try Next's experimental offline detection and retries in preview first. Then add Serwist for offline screens and cached audio and pictures, plus a local queue for weigh-and-pay records: send in the background on Chrome, or on next open in other browsers. A weighing must never be lost.
- **Assisted mode: 'Book for someone' and 'Call me'** (pilot): People in Bangalore slums often use phones through a helper (CHI 2010), and a live operator was up to 10 times more accurate than text (TOCHI 2011). Let a kabadiwala or Saathi book a pickup for a household, which the household confirms with its SMS code. Add a 'Call me' button that reaches the admin during the pilot.
- **Bengaluru language testing, including Urdu (right-to-left), plus a picture recognition test** (pilot): Bengaluru is 42% Kannada, 16% Tamil, 14% Telugu and 13% Urdu speakers. Test the core flows in all four plus Hindi and English with native speakers. Check that line spacing doesn't clip Indian scripts, that arrows mirror in Urdu, and how numbers sit inside Urdu text. Before fixing the material pictures, run a quick test with 5 users to check they recognise each one.
- **Automatic accessibility and page-weight checks in CI** (pilot): Add automated accessibility checks (axe) to the Playwright end-to-end tests at 360x800, the most common Indian screen, and fail the build on contrast, button-size or missing-label errors. Keep household and kabadiwala pages under about 365 KB of compressed JavaScript, the 3-second budget on a mid-range phone, and serve images as WebP or AVIF.
- **Publish a Luma.Green design kit and an open set of material pictures** (scale): Package the colours, the low-literacy building blocks and an openly licensed set of material pictures as a shadcn registry. Partner yards, recyclers, manufacturers and a future mobile app can then reuse the same icons, colours and words, which is the practical way for the industry to adopt Luma.Green's conventions. Add voice input later (on-device recognition where available, then dialect models trained on Vaani-style data).

## Risks

- Licence terms change over time. unDraw's terms were revised in 2026, and Storyset requires credit 'as stated from time to time'. Record the date each licence was checked and re-check before launch.
- unDraw and DrawKit forbid using their art to train or feed AI tools. Generating 'Indian versions' of their illustrations with AI would breach the licence, and copyright in AI-generated art is unclear.
- Share-alike licences (OpenMoji, CC BY-SA files on Wikimedia) could require Luma.Green's edited pictures to be released under the same licence. Scattered CC BY icons build up credits that are easy to forget.
- Pictures of kabadiwalas and waste workers can slide into stereotypes (dirt, poverty). Depict them as skilled professionals, as the name Saathi intends, and review the art with pilot users.
- Synthetic voices may mispronounce trade words (raddi, bhangar) or local place names, or use the wrong dialect, which erodes trust. Speaking live details adds cost and delay.
- Next's offline detection is experimental and Next says not to use it in production yet. Offline caching can leave users stuck on an old version of the app, and Background Sync works only in Chrome and Edge.
- Bigger buttons and fewer words mean more screens. If the order of steps isn't thought through, users end up scrolling, which NN/g found hurts low-literacy readers.
- Switching to international digits may annoy some Bengali or Marathi users who expect their own script's digits. Test it before rolling it out.
- Shared phones: family members can see code SMSs and tracking links, so tracking pages must show little personal data.
- The extra auto-fill line makes each code SMS longer. In Kannada or Hindi, where one SMS holds only 70 characters, that may mean two SMSs per code and higher cost. Adding the line after DLT approval means approving the templates again.

## Open questions

- Which languages do the actual pilot kabadiwalas and households in Bengaluru read and speak, and how many can read numbers but not words? A 5-person check would settle it.
- Do Bengali, Marathi and Urdu pilot users prefer international digits or their own script's digits for prices and weights?
- Do users recognise materials faster from real photos or from drawn pictures? This needs a quick recognition test before the material tiles are fixed.
- Do the bin colours in the new Solid Waste Management Rules (wet, dry, sanitary, special care) clash with the planned material colours? Not checked here.
- Which text-to-speech service for the pilot: Google's Chirp 3 HD, the free Indic Parler-TTS, or Bhashini/Sarvam? Pricing and startup access to Bhashini were not checked.
- Does the Convex client keep unsent changes after the app reloads offline, or does the weigh-and-pay queue need to be stored on the phone by us? Next's automatic retries cover its own form actions, not Convex updates.
- What are the licence terms of IconScout, Blush and Icons8? Their pages blocked our access.
- Which Indian accessibility rules (the RPwD Act 2016, the Indian standard IS 17802, the government's GIGW 3.0 guidelines) apply to a private platform like Luma.Green? Not checked here.
- What budget and timeline are there for commissioning a custom set of Indian clothing and props for the illustrations?
