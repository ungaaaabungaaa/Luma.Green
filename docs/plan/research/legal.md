# legal

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ Keeping money off the platform (ADR 0009) keeps Luma.Green outside GST e-commerce TCS and RBI payment-aggregator licensing for the Bengaluru pilot. Escrow would switch both on: 0.5% TCS with a GSTR-8 due by the 10th of every month, and money could only be held by an RBI-authorised aggregator, which itself needs ₹15 crore net worth. The amended E-Commerce Rules add grievance, seller-disclosure and dark-pattern-audit duties from 1 Jan 2027 whether or not escrow ever starts.

# Compliance checklists: Luma.Green and each user

> **Draft, 29 Sep 2026. This is not legal advice.** Have a lawyer and a tax adviser review it before real money or real users are involved.

Each row gives one duty, the number that triggers it, the law, the Luma.Green feature that does the work, and when to build it.

- **Build column:** P = prototype-now, Pi = pilot, S = scale.
- Every number in the Trigger column belongs in the admin-editable **Rulebook**, not in code.

## Recommendations

- **Rulebook: every legal threshold, rate and deadline stored as data the admin can edit, each with an effective-from date. This covers the ₹50,000 e-way bill limit, the ₹2.5 lakh limit and 2% rate for GST TDS, ₹5 crore for e-invoicing, ₹40 lakh for GST registration, 0.5% e-commerce TCS, 2% TCS on scrap, 12- or 24-month scale re-verification, 5- or 10-year consent validity and the gig-fee caps** (prototype-now): At least six of these numbers changed between Jul 2024 and Jan 2027:
- e-commerce TCS rate (10 Jul 2024);
- metal-scrap reverse charge and TDS (10 Oct 2024);
- 30-day self-invoice deadline (1 Nov 2024);
- TCS on scrap (1 Apr 2026);
- Legal Metrology penalties (1 May 2026);
- E-Commerce amendment (1 Jan 2027).
  Keeping them as data meets the founders' 'easy to modify' requirement, and every other compliance feature reads from the same table.
- **Auto-invoices that pick the right document for each hand-off from the seller's GST flag and the material code: a household receipt, an RCM self-invoice with payment voucher, a tax invoice with a 2% TDS note, a TCS line, and later an IRN (e-invoice) through a GST provider** (prototype-now): The documents differ at each step:
- Household to kabadiwala needs only a receipt, because it is not a business supply (CGST s.7).
- An unregistered kabadiwala selling metal to a registered yard needs the yard's reverse-charge self-invoice within 30 days.
- Metal between two registered parties above ₹2.5 lakh needs 2% GST TDS.
- Scrap sales carry income-tax TCS, 2% from 1 Apr 2026.
- From 1 Jan 2027 the seller's name must appear in the same font size as the platform's.
  Getting this right automatically removes a common scrap-trade tax error and makes a strong investor demo. It also settles the docs' open question: require a GSTIN from any yard, recycler or manufacturer that buys metal.
- **Weighing-scale records: for each scale, its type and capacity, Legal Metrology certificate number, stamp photo and next due date, with a reminder 30 days ahead, a 'Scale verified until …' badge on booking, and a photo of the reading on every weigh-and-pay** (prototype-now): Every scale used in trade must be verified and stamped. Electronic scales and weighbridges are re-verified every 12 months and beam scales every 24. An unverified scale drew a ₹2,000–₹10,000 fine on a first offence, and since 1 May 2026 improvement notices come first. A visible verified scale is the strongest proof of the fair price that households are promised.
- **E-way bill helper: a yes/no checker (value, vehicle type, registration status, distance), then Part A pre-filled from the trade record for the portal, and later a GST-provider API** (prototype-now): Rule 138 requires an e-way bill for motor-vehicle loads over ₹50,000, including a registered buyer's inward load from an unregistered seller. Handcarts and cycles are exempt. An e-commerce operator may fill Part A on the consignor's authorisation, and each bill is valid for one day per 200 km, so the helper can also show a countdown.
- **Document vault and public compliance passport: the GSTIN (checked on the GST portal), KSPCB consent with colour, number and validity (checked on XGN), trade licence, Udyam, CPCB EPR registrations and PAN, shown as badges such as 'Consent valid till 2031' or 'GST verified'** (prototype-now): KSPCB consents run 5 years (Red and Orange) or 10 years (Green) and can be checked publicly on XGN. From 1 Jan 2027 sellers must disclose a GSTIN or Udyam number. RBI's small-merchant KYC for escrow needs PAN, a shop check and one ID. The vault turns the admin's manual checks into a visible trust signal for investors and buyers, and most of the data is already collected in onboarding.
- **Compliance calendar per organisation: GSTR-7 and GSTR-8 by the 10th, consent expiry 30 days ahead, scale re-verification, trade licence renewal, the quarterly Karnataka gig-welfare fee, 45-day payment deadlines to micro and small suppliers, and the yearly dark-pattern self-audit** (pilot): Most penalties in this sector come from missed dates rather than wrong rates. Examples: the TDS return by the 10th, consents lapsing after 5 or 10 years, 12-month scale re-verification, compound interest at three times the bank rate after 45 days, and quarterly gig fees. Reminders built on the Rulebook cost little once it exists.
- **Grievance desk and a pack of legal pages: a named Grievance Officer, a ticket system with 24-hour and 48-hour acknowledgement timers, the legal name and address block, a ranking explanation, sponsored labels, the National Consumer Helpline convergence application, terms in English and Eighth-Schedule languages, and a yearly reminder to users** (pilot): The IT Rules require acknowledgement within 24 hours and resolution within 15 days. The E-Commerce Rules require acknowledgement within 48 hours, a copy of the complaint and redress within one month. The 2026 amendment makes the National Consumer Helpline convergence programme, address disclosure and the dark-pattern certificate mandatory from 1 Jan 2027. These pages need to exist before real users arrive.
- **Saathi work register and gig-law pack: days worked per engager, joins and exits, payouts recorded, export for Shram Suvidha and the Karnataka welfare board, a welfare-fee calculator (1%, capped per vehicle type), and a Saathi agreement covering pay basis, the right to refuse tasks, 14 days' notice and a dispute flow** (pilot): The central rules of 8 May 2026 require aggregators to register, upload workers within 45 days and report joins and exits in real time, with benefits after 90 or 120 days of work. Karnataka requires board registration within 45 days and a 1% fee capped at ₹0.50–₹1.00 per e-marketplace job, paid quarterly. Its fines run ₹5,000–₹1 lakh plus 12% interest.
- **Escrow only through an RBI-authorised payment aggregator's marketplace (split-payment) product, with an 'e-commerce operator switch' that turns on TCS collection, the GSTR-8 export and seller registration or enrolment checks** (scale): Licensing itself as a payment aggregator needs ₹15 crore net worth, rising to ₹25 crore. Aggregators cannot run marketplaces, and a marketplace may only take payments for onboarded sellers. Escrow also makes Luma.Green a GST e-commerce operator: 0.5% TCS, GSTR-8 by the 10th and an annual statement by 31 Dec. Sellers would then need GST registration or a PAN-based enrolment number, which is limited to intra-state sales. The pilot should keep only recording payments, and households and small kabadiwalas should stay out of escrow.
- **Privacy centre ready for DPDP: a notice for each role, a consent log, rights requests (30-day target, 90-day legal limit), a breach runbook (CERT-In within 6 hours, the Board within 72 hours) and log retention of at least one year** (pilot): The core DPDP duties apply from about 13 May 2027 and penalties reach ₹250 crore. Building to the standard now, as the repo's data-protection page already plans, avoids a rebuild and reassures investors.
- **Provenance and prohibited-items guard: a booking checklist and photo review that flags utility cables, manhole covers, lead-acid batteries and e-waste; hazardous categories sent only to authorised recyclers; the seller's phone, photos and weight kept for each purchase; matching terms in the user agreement** (pilot): The IT Rules require the user agreement to bar unlawful content. In Karnataka, recycling lead-acid battery plates and e-waste components is a Red-category activity. Stolen metal is the trade's best-known legal risk. A purchase trail protects honest kabadiwalas and the platform.

## Risks

- Escrow switches on several regimes at once. Funds must go through an RBI-authorised aggregator. Luma.Green must register for GST as an e-commerce operator, collect 0.5% TCS and file GSTR-8 monthly. Sellers must be GST-registered or enrolled with their PAN. E-commerce TDS may also apply. Together these could push unregistered kabadiwalas off the platform, so households and small kabadiwalas should stay outside escrow.
- Receiving any trade money into Luma.Green's own bank or UPI account, even briefly, looks like unauthorised payment aggregation, and authorisation needs ₹15 crore net worth.
- A 'Verified scale' badge will show that many kabadiwala scales are unstamped. Making it a hard requirement in the pilot could empty the supply side, so treat it as a nudge with a deadline.
- Stolen or hazardous scrap (utility cables, manhole covers, lead-acid batteries, e-waste) passing through the platform exposes users to charges of receiving stolen goods and to pollution law, and exposes Luma.Green to reputational damage.
- Saathi classification is uncertain. If Luma.Green counts as an 'aggregator', central registration (within 45 days) and Karnataka welfare-fee duties apply even though kabadiwalas pay the Saathis. Misjudging this risks fines of ₹5,000–₹1 lakh plus 12% interest.
- AI price estimates shown to households could be treated as misleading if the final weighed prices are routinely lower. Label estimates clearly and publish their accuracy.
- Hard-coded thresholds will go out of date: at least six legal numbers changed between Jul 2024 and Jan 2027.
- DPDP penalties reach ₹250 crore. Household photos sent to an overseas model provider and data hosted in the EU need named processors, contracts, and logs kept for one year. MeitY has also considered shortening deadlines for some companies.
- Binding legal text in 12 languages invites translation errors. Arabic is not an Eighth-Schedule language, so the binding version should be English or an Eighth-Schedule language.
- Investor demos must not use real GSTINs, consent numbers or business names copied from the public XGN register.

## Open questions

- Does e-commerce operator TDS (0.1%, the old s.194-O) apply to Luma.Green when buyers pay sellers directly, and which section of the Income-tax Act 2025 now carries it?
- Are households 'consumers' under the Consumer Protection Act when they sell scrap and get a free pickup? That decides whether the E-Commerce Rules' grievance duties cover the household leg (assume yes).
- Is Luma.Green an 'aggregator' for Saathi work under the Social Security Code's Seventh Schedule and the Karnataka Act if Saathis are paid by kabadiwalas, not by the platform? If so, what is the welfare fee calculated on?
- What is the status of the reported 2026 Karnataka High Court challenge to the gig welfare fee?
- After MeitY's January 2026 consultations, were the DPDP deadlines (about 13 May 2027) shortened for any class of company?
- Have amendments to the IT Rules in 2025–26 changed the 36-hour takedown and the 24-hour / 15-day grievance timelines? The version checked was consolidated to April 2023.
- Did the Finance Act 2026 enact 2% TCS on scrap? Which sellers are covered, and does the exemption for buyers who use scrap in manufacturing survive in the 2025 Act?
- What penalty now applies to an unverified scale after the Jan Vishwas changes in force from 1 May 2026, and does Karnataka's enforcement rule require yearly re-verification for every instrument?
- Do plain kabadiwala shops that only collect and store scrap need any KSPCB consent or authorisation under the waste rules? They are not in KSPCB's category lists.
- Does Bengaluru police require scrap dealers to hold a licence or keep purchase registers?
- Should the e-way bill helper use a Karnataka-specific intra-state limit, or is ₹50,000 correct for all intra-state movement? Confirm on the e-way bill portal.
