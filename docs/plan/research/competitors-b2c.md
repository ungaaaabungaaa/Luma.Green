# competitors-b2c

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ Household scrap pickup is proven but thin-margin. Recykal closed its ~0.5-million-download consumer app because each pickup took 45–60 minutes, and the market leader, The Kabadiwala, already runs a partner-kabadiwala marketplace in Bengaluru (Scrapr, 5,000+ kabadiwalas). Luma.Green has to win on reliability, photo estimates, prices shown before booking and a Kannada-first app, not on the marketplace idea itself.

## Recommendations

- **Show nearby kabadiwalas side by side on the estimate, with distance and their price for these materials, and tag the best price** (prototype-now): Scrapr already lets households choose a partner by distance and price, and published Bengaluru rates differ by up to 3 times. Showing each verified shop's rate card for the photographed materials (the fallback table where a shop has set none) answers open question 3 in pricing.md. It also makes the 'latest prices' promise visible in the investor demo, using data Luma.Green already stores.
- **Reliability loop: auto-accept, re-offer the booking to the next kabadiwala after a timeout, and a live 'who is coming, when, on the way' screen** (prototype-now): No-shows, slow assignment and stuck requests are the top complaints about The Kabadiwala and EpiCircle. Demoing a booking that moves to the next kabadiwala after the timeout (open question 8), with SMS updates, shows investors that Luma.Green has designed for the problem that hurts the incumbents.
- **Digital receipt after weighing: kg × rate for each material, cash or UPI, estimate against actual, and a kg and CO2e impact line, shareable by link** (prototype-now): Honest weighing and instant payment are what households value, and every leader advertises digital scales and bills. Showing estimate against actual builds trust in the AI and feeds the pilot measures. The impact line sets up the carbon module.
- **A public, search-indexed 'Bengaluru scrap rates today' page built from the dated floor and fallback tables, with history** (prototype-now): City rate pages are how The Kabadiwala, ScrapKabadi and ScrapRates.in attract search traffic. Luma.Green's dated tables already hold this data, so the page costs little. It gives kabadiwalas a reference price and shows investors the planned live-prices module. The numbers should be labelled as indicative.
- **Show each kabadiwala's minimum before booking, offer 'too small for a pickup? drop it off' with the shop code, and never charge a surprise fee** (pilot): Minimums differ (15 kg, ₹999, 'only with other items'), and a ₹30 surprise fee led to a public complaint. Showing the minimum at booking and sending small loads to drop-off turns a sore point into a feature. It also uses kabadiwalas without a vehicle, who stay visible for drop-offs.
- **Let each kabadiwala mark every material as 'buys', 'takes free' or 'doesn't take', and show it on the estimate** (pilot): The Kabadiwala lists some items at ₹0 instead of refusing them, ScrapUncle refuses thermocol, wood and ceramics, and about 82% of the dry waste reaching Bengaluru's collection centres is low-value. A three-way flag on the existing rate card sets expectations and avoids arguments at the door. Later it lets leftovers be sent to DWCCs.
- **Add a WhatsApp support number (tap to chat) and a 'share to WhatsApp' button on the tracking page now; build the full WhatsApp booking bot later** (pilot): Every leader takes bookings or support over WhatsApp or phone, and slow support is a top complaint. A tap-to-chat number and a share button need no WhatsApp API work and cover most of the value until the planned bot is built.
- **Self-serve cancel and reschedule, a two-tap rating after each pickup, and a reliability score for each kabadiwala that affects who gets offered bookings first** (pilot): One review describes a request that got stuck and could not be cancelled, and ScrapUncle has added a review for each pickup. Ranking nearby shops partly on their acceptance and completion record from pilot data stops unreliable dealers from damaging the brand.
- **Apartment pickup days: a kabadiwala opens a time slot at a housing complex and residents book into it** (pilot): Scrapdeal's 500+ housing societies with 80–90% repeat customers, and Bintix's model of about 41,000 households signed up through their communities, show apartments are the efficient household channel. Grouping pickups raises the kilograms per trip, which was the problem that sank Uzed. Bengaluru's apartment resident associations (RWAs) are an easy way in for the pilot.
- **Make recycle points an impact count (kg recycled, CO2e) with no cash value in the pilot, add referral bonuses later, and never offer gold or 'returns'** (pilot): Competitors' rewards are coupons and impact stats, and no source ties them to customers coming back. EpiCircle's '11–15% returns' in gold and silver looks like a financial product. ADR 0009 keeps money off the platform, so points with a cash value would need payments first. This settles open question 6 simply.
- **Send household e-waste to authorised recyclers and brand take-back schemes, with income tied to recycling compliance (EPR) later** (scale): Bengaluru households already have about 10 doorstep e-waste options, most of them free brand take-backs, so e-waste earns them little. Survivors such as Crapbin and ScrapUncle make their money by processing e-waste. Luma.Green should earn through its recycler network rather than compete on the price paid to households.
- **A data product for brands and EPR compliance bodies: anonymised material and packaging data, collected only with consent** (scale): Bintix pays for its household service by selling consumption analysis from the packaging of about 41,000 households. Luma.Green's photos and weighed receipts produce similar data at every step of the chain and could become a second revenue line. This needs explicit consent under India's data-protection law and must keep the 90-day photo deletion rule.

## Risks

- Direct overlap with the leader: The Kabadiwala's Scrapr already runs a partner-kabadiwala marketplace in 25+ Bengaluru neighbourhoods and claims 5,000+ kabadiwalas nationally. Investors who know it will discount a 'marketplace for kabadiwalas' pitch unless Luma.Green leads with the photo estimate, the Kannada-first app for low-literacy kabadiwalas and the full chain.
- Thin margins: household loads are small (each Uzed order took 45–60 minutes, and the app closed). If pilot kabadiwalas find pickups unprofitable they will stop accepting them, which is already a stop condition in pilot.md.
- Reliability depends on others: independent kabadiwalas' no-shows and refusals become Luma.Green's bad reviews, as The Kabadiwala's App Store reviews show.
- Price expectations: some sites advertise newspaper at ₹14–16/kg against ₹10–13 elsewhere. If the estimate or the fallback table is higher than what shops pay at the door, households feel cheated. If the minimum prices are above market, shops refuse bookings.
- Rewards and money: points with a cash value, or gold-linked 'returns' like EpiCircle's, raise payment and financial-product questions that ADR 0009 deliberately avoids in the pilot.
- Privacy: photos of homes, exact addresses and phone numbers are sensitive. Any data product needs explicit consent and anonymisation under the DPDP Act.
- Shutdown history: Junkart, Kabadi-Xpress and Recykal's Uzed are gone. Investors will ask how often households sell and how many come back, which is hard to show in a short pilot because households sell scrap only every few weeks or months.
- Data quality: most competitor numbers (users, kg, downloads) are self-reported or Tracxn estimates, and Play Store pages could not be read, so treat the matrix as indicative.

## Open questions

- What does Scrapr charge partner kabadiwalas (subscription or commission), and how many of its 5,000+ kabadiwalas are active in Bengaluru? Its pricing page returned 404.
- Is ScrapUncle actually operating in Bengaluru? Its rate card lists Bengaluru, but its January 2026 funding news mentions only Delhi NCR.
- What minimum load (in kg or ₹) makes a home pickup worth the trip for a Bengaluru kabadiwala, and what share of households reach it? To be measured in the pilot.
- When shown both, do households pick the nearest kabadiwala or the one with the best price?
- How often does a typical Bengaluru household have enough scrap to sell (monthly, quarterly)? This sets the repeat-use numbers investors will expect.
- Can Luma.Green hand low-value materials to waste-picker-run DWCCs or Hasiru Dala, and on what terms?
- How many DWCCs operate today under the Greater Bengaluru Authority? The latest figure found (166 working of 198) is from 2019.
- Do the Android versions of competitor apps offer Indian languages? Only the iPhone listings were checked, and they show English only.
