# business

_Research brief, 29 September 2026. Headline:_ In Indian recycling, the money has followed regulation rather than households: Recykal's revenue went from ₹1.8 crore (FY19) to ₹21.2 crore (FY20) after it moved from a scrap marketplace to EPR compliance. The SWM Rules 2026 (in force since 1 April 2026) and the rise in required recycled content for rigid plastic packaging (30% in FY2025-26, 60% from FY2028-29) make Luma.Green's weighed hand-off records the thing to sell, not commissions on household pickups.

# Luma.Green: investor narrative (one-page outline)

_Draft for the investor prototype, 29 Sep 2026. Figures marked (H) are our guesses, to be tested in the pilot. Every other figure comes from a source in the research notes._

**In one line.** Luma.Green records India's informal recycling chain. Every hand-off from household to manufacturer is weighed, priced and traceable, in 12 languages, on a basic smartphone.

## 1. Problem

- India generates **1,85,195 tonnes a day** of municipal waste. **38,729 tonnes a day (21%)** is neither processed nor landfilled; nobody can say where it went (CPCB 2023-24).
- Karnataka generates **12,140 tonnes a day**. Bengaluru separates only **53–55%** at source.
- Recyclables do move, through kabadiwalas and yards, but on cash, guessed weights and phone calls. There are no published prices, no records and no proof of origin.

## 2. Why now

- **SWM Rules 2026, in force since 1 Apr 2026.** Anyone generating 100 kg or more a day, including residential societies, must register on a CPCB portal, file returns by 30 June and buy certificates for the waste they generate (EBWGR certificates). Every dry-waste sorter must register with the local body.
- **Plastic EPR.** Rigid packaging must contain **30% recycled plastic in FY2025-26, rising to 60% from FY2028-29**. Certificates must match a recycler's real capacity and carry GST data.
- **Regulated certificate markets.** E-waste EPR certificates trade on a government portal at **30–100%** of the penalty for non-compliance. What is scarce is proof, not certificates.
- UPI payments, cheap photo-recognition AI and first-time smartphone users make a three-tap household flow possible.

## 3. Product (working prototype)

- **Household:** photo, then AI estimate, then pickup, then paid in cash or UPI.
- **Kabadiwala:** booking requests, their own rate card above an admin floor price, weigh-and-record, stock.
- **Yard, recycler, manufacturer:** lots to buy and sell, and a trace view.
- **Saathi:** short paid jobs.
- **Admin:** every business checked by hand.

- **Why no commission from households.** A 20 kg basket pays the household about ₹250 at Bengaluru rates. A 5% fee is about ₹12, less than the cost of a pickup that takes 45–60 minutes, and cash at the door makes it easy to skip the app.
- **Example of a mature city (H).** 500 societies at ₹1,000 plus 50 yards at ₹3,000 plus 1% of 3,000 tonnes a month at ₹25/kg comes to about **₹14 lakh a month (about ₹1.7 crore a year)**, before EPR, data and referral income.

## 5. Why we win

- **Equip kabadiwalas, don't replace them.** Competitors in Bengaluru run their own pickup service. We give the city's existing kabadiwalas the tools and connect them to buyers further up the chain.
- **Three effects that build on each other.** Busy local clusters come first. Every weighed receipt then sharpens the AI estimates and the price index. Finally, open material codes and a receipt format that others adopt make us the standard; ReMA's scrap specifications are the global example.
- **Trust as a rule of the platform.** Participants are verified, weighing follows a set standard and the record of each lot is unbroken. Regulators, brands and lenders can accept that as evidence.

## 8. How we expand

- **The unit is a cluster:** 1 yard, 15–30 kabadiwalas within about 3 km, 2,000–5,000 households and 3–5 societies. A city is 5–10 clusters.
- **A new city is data, not code:** floor and fallback price tables, local material names, pollution-board document types, local-body registration, one operations lead, one anchor recycler and one waste-picker organisation as a partner.
- **Order**, based on CPCB tonnage and the languages already built:
  1. Bengaluru
  2. Mysuru and Hubballi (same language and pollution board)
  3. Hyderabad, Chennai and Pune/Mumbai (state totals of 11,714, 17,843 and 24,299 tonnes a day)
  4. Kolkata, Ahmedabad and Delhi NCR

## 9. Making our rules the industry's rules

- **Luma Material Codes and the hand-off receipt format**, published under the CC BY 4.0 licence. Each code has names in 12 languages, photos, and its matching ReMA grade, HSN tax code and CPCB category.
- **Open to change:** anyone can propose changes and every version is numbered.
- **Advisory group:** kabadiwalas, yards, recyclers and a waste-picker organisation. Once others use the codes, hand them to a neutral body to look after.
- **Weighing standard:** a government-stamped scale, a photo of the reading and confirmation from both sides.
- **Luma Verified badge:** shown on checked businesses and withdrawn if they break the rules.

## 10. Risks and how we handle them

- **Trading off the platform:** no commission on household pickups.
- **Regulated certificate markets:** we sell evidence, never certificates.
- **Carbon:** reported as an impact measure only, until an approved method covers recycling.
- **Karnataka gig-worker law:** the 1–5% welfare fee is built into Saathi pricing.
- **Lending rules:** referral fees paid by the lender, and we never hold loan money.
- **Demo data:** always labelled as demo.

## Recommendations

- **Traction dashboard: extend the admin pilot-numbers page into a view for investors** (prototype-now): a16z's marketplace measures (match rate, time to match, take rate, retention by cohort and by city) are what seed investors ask for, and the events table from ADR 0012 already captures most of them. Show kg traced by material, recorded rupee value of trades, completion rate, median time to accept, how close the AI's weight estimate is to the weighed kg, repeat households, active verified businesses, 'shadow revenue' (what fees would have earned if switched on) and CO2e avoided. Label the prototype clearly as demo data.
- **Trace view: one short code or QR per lot, linking household receipts to kabadiwala stock, the yard lot and the recycler batch** (prototype-now): It shows the core idea, recording every hand-off, on one screen. Plastic EPR certificates must match real capacity and carry GST data, and the SWM Rules 2026 require registrations and annual returns. Metycle's batch-level record backs a $150M credit facility. Export the chain as CSV or JSON with GST number, material code, weight and date so it can feed CPCB returns.
- **Open material codes v0.1: an admin-editable table, a public page and a CSV/JSON download under the CC BY 4.0 licence** (prototype-now): Standards-based network effects are the strongest kind (NFX ranks them 2nd of 16), and ReMA's specifications show that a free list of grades that anyone can propose changes to becomes the trade's common language. Each code carries names in 12 languages, a photo, a unit, the matching ReMA grade, the HSN tax code and the CPCB category. Because it is a table, it can be changed without code.
- **Pricing plans stored as data, with 'shadow revenue'** (prototype-now): Keep every fee at ₹0 during the pilot: Gurley's point is that low fees win, and cash at the door makes high fees leak. But store plans per city and per stream (pickup fee, kabadiwala Pro, yard workspace, fee on business-to-business lots, society plan) and calculate what each would have earned. Investors see the model, users see no friction, and switching a fee on is a data change.
- **Trust norms: a public 'Luma Verified' badge plus a weighing standard in weigh-and-record** (prototype-now): Verification is already done by hand. Show the checks passed (ID and selfie, pollution-board consent PDF, GST if given, date verified) as a badge that can be withdrawn. For each weighing, require a photo of the scale, confirmation from both sides and a way to flag a dispute. This turns the pilot's 'estimated vs weighed kg' measure into evidence, and it is the start of a certification others can refer to.
- **Accounts for apartment societies and other bulk generators under the SWM Rules 2026** (pilot): Since 1 Apr 2026, anyone generating 100 kg or more of waste a day, including residential societies, must register on CPCB's portal, file returns by 30 June and buy EBWGR certificates. A society account would give scheduled dry-waste pickups by a verified kabadiwala, monthly weighed statements and a yearly summary for the return. That is recurring revenue driven by compliance (our guess: ₹500–2,000 a month) and it brings many households at once. Start with 3–5 Bengaluru societies as design partners.
- **Yard workspace: a light software product for yards** (pilot): Yards need purchase receipts, a stock ledger, sale lots, GST invoice fields and a record of their Rule 9(1) registration with the local body. Small-business software keeps 60–80% of users after 6 months, which makes this the most predictable revenue. Free in the pilot; our guess is ₹1,500–5,000 a month later.
- **Bengaluru price index: weekly median prices per material from receipts and yards' buying prices, shown next to the admin floor price** (pilot): Households already compare published rates (Scrapuncle lists ₹11–13/kg for newspaper and ₹7–8/kg for plastic). A neutral index built from real weighed receipts becomes a reference price and a public search-friendly page. It improves as more trades are recorded, and later it can be a paid data feed or report for recyclers and manufacturers.
- **Saathi payout ledger that includes Karnataka's welfare fee** (pilot): Karnataka's gig-worker law sets a welfare fee of 1–5% on each payout, requires aggregators to register within 45 days and to report payments. Record every Saathi payout and its fee from the first day, and keep paid Saathi jobs switched off until the fee rate for Luma.Green's category is known.
- **Evidence packs for registered recyclers and PROs, covering EPR and recycled content** (scale): Rigid packaging must contain 30% recycled plastic in FY2025-26 and 60% from FY2028-29. EPR certificates trade only on government platforms, at 30–100% of the non-compliance penalty. So sell verified chain-of-custody evidence priced per tonne, and never trade certificates. Add plastic-credit projects later through a partner registered with Verra.
- **Working-capital loan referrals to an RBI-regulated lender, based on platform receipts** (scale): Kabadiwalas pay households in cash, and yards wait to be paid by recyclers, so both need working capital. Their receipts are evidence a lender can use. Under RBI's digital lending rules the lender pays the referral fee and money moves only between lender and borrower, so Luma.Green earns without holding any funds.
- **Rooftop solar leads under PM Surya Ghar, for households and yards** (scale): The scheme has ₹75,021 crore for 1 crore homes, with subsidies up to ₹78,000, and households already trust the app after a paid pickup. Pass leads to approved vendors for a fee per installation. This is a low-effort add-on, not core business.

## Risks

- Trading off the platform: after one booking through the app, a household and kabadiwala can deal directly in cash, and any per-pickup fee speeds this up (Gurley).
- Weak household unit economics: about ₹250 per 20 kg pickup, and 45–60 minutes of reverse logistics per order (Recykal). Pickups alone will never pay for the company.
- Regulated certificate markets: EPR certificates are exchanged on government platforms at 30–100% of the non-compliance penalty, and processors who give false data are barred for a year. A partner's fraud would taint Luma.Green's records.
- Carbon expectations: no Indian offset method covers dry recyclables, and voluntary plastic credits are small (40,000+ credits worldwide). Do not put carbon revenue in the base-case model shown to investors.
- Gig-worker law: Karnataka's 1–5% welfare fee, registration within 45 days and duty to share data may apply to Saathi jobs.
- Loan referrals need a regulated lending partner and strict compliance with RBI rules: the lender pays the fee, and no loan money passes through Luma.Green.
- Competition in Bengaluru from companies that run their own pickup service (Scrapuncle, The Kabadiwala) and from EPR service providers.
- Investor demo risk: the prototype runs on demo data, and unlabelled numbers could be read as real traction.
- Changing rules: the plastic EPR rules have been amended several times since 2022, so re-check the recycled-content percentages before putting them in a deck.
- Adoption of the standard: if the material codes look proprietary or change unpredictably, competitors and regulators will not adopt them.

## Open questions

- Under the SWM Rules 2026, who issues EBWGR certificates and at what price? Can a network of kabadiwalas and yards supply them to Bengaluru societies? (We need the text of the 27 Jan 2026 notification.)
- What are kabadiwalas' real gross margins per material, and will they pay a monthly subscription or only per lead?
- What are typical kabadiwala-to-yard lot sizes, how many days of credit are given, and who transports the material? This sizes both the business-to-business fee and the need for financing.
- Has Karnataka's gig-worker Bill been passed and brought into force, and what fee rate would apply to Saathi jobs?
- Will Luma.Green count as an e-commerce operator under GST (and so have to collect tax at source) once trades or escrow go live?
- Do CPCB's EPR and SWM portals accept bulk uploads or API connections from outside record systems?
- Which neutral body should look after the material codes in the long run: an industry association, a BIS committee, or a foundation?
- What are the current recycled-content requirements after any 2024–2026 amendments to the plastic rules?
